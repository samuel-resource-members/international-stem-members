Caryophyllales is the carnation order of dicotyledonous flowering plants. It is a diverse order that includes
trees, shrubs, lianas, mangroves, stem or leaf succulents, annuals, and even carnivorous plants. Many
members of the order are ecologically specialized to tolerate extreme environments. In the following
experiment we are going to investigate ecophysiological features of five members of caryophyllales order
in response to different habitats.

Protocol 2.1. Measuring the soil CaCO  content
 
To investigate the total concentration of all salts in soil, we measure CaCO  content of soil washouts by
conducting volumetric assay. “Solution 1 for CaCO  measurement” contains an indicator that turns blue
when there is no free Ca
 in the mixture. “Solution 2 for CaCO  measurement” contains compound X (4
mM) that forms 1:1 complex with Ca
 ions.
1. Add 10 mL of the soil washout to the beaker. (Wash the beaker with distilled water before this step, if
necessary). 
 
2. Add 2 drops of the “Solution 1 for CaCO  measurement” and mix. If the solution is blue, the CaCO
concentration is ≤1mg/L. If the solution is red, then proceed to next step
 
3. Using syringe, add “CaCO  solution 2” to the mixture until the colour turns blue. Shake the beaker
while adding the solution. Write the volume of “Solution 2 for CaCO  measurement”” consumed and Ca
concentration of soil washouts in Question 2: Table 1 of the answer sheet (Ca molar mass = 40
g/mole).
