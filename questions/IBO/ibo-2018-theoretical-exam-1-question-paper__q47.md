```text
Senescence
Organismal senescence is the aging of the whole organism. It is a widespread phenomenon in nature 
but the exact etiology is still largely unclear. An evolutionary theory was proposed by George C. 
Williams which involves the following assumptions: 
1.
Soma (referring to all somatic cells in the body) is essential for reproductive success, but it
does not pass on to the offspring.
2.
Alleles are affected by Natural selection.
3.
Pleiotropic genes can have opposite effects on fitness at different ages or somatic
environments.
4.
The probability of reproduction decreases with increasing age.
Williams suggested the following example: Perhaps a gene was selected for because it codes for 
calcium deposition in bones, which promotes juvenile survival, but the same gene promotes calcium 
deposition in arteries. 
Lower adult death rates can be associated with lower rates of senescence,
according to the theory.
Where there is a sex difference, the sex with higher rate of decrease in
fecundity after maturation (but with equal death rate to the other sex) should
undergo the more rapid senescence, according to the theory.
The frequencies of an allele in a gene that behaves similar to the
aforementioned calcium deposition gene in a cohort will be smallest at early
age and greatest at late age.
This model views senescence as an adaptation.
Indicate if each of the following statements is true or false:
```
