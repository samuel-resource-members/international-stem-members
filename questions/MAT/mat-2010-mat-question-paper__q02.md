2. For ALL APPLICANTS.

     Suppose that a, b, c are integers such that
                              a√2 + b = c√3.

(i) By squaring both sides of the equation, show that a = b = c = 0.
[You may assume that √2, √3 and   2/3 are all irrational numbers. An irrational
number is one which cannot be written in the form p/q where p and q are integers.]

(ii) Suppose now that m, n, M, N are integers such that the distance from the point
(m, n) to √2, √3  equals the distance from (M, N) to √2, √3  .

Show that m = M and n = N.

     Given real numbers a, b and a positive number r, let N (a, b, r) be the number of
