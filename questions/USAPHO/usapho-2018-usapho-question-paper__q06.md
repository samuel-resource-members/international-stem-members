```text
Question B3

Radiation pressure from the sun is responsible for cleaning out the inner solar system of small
particles.

   a. The force of radiation on a spherical particle of radius r is given by

                               F = PQπr2

     where P  is the radiation pressure and Q is a dimensionless quality factor that depends on
     the relative size of the particle r and the wavelength of light λ. Throughout this problem
     assume that the sun emits a single wavelength λmax; unless told otherwise, leave your answers
      in terms of symbolic variables.

             i. Given that the total power radiated from the sun is given by L⊙, ﬁnd an expression for
          the radiation pressure a distance R from the sun.
                                                                                                    Fradiation
            ii. Assuming that the particle has a density ρ, derive an expression for the ratio
                                                                                                   Fgravity
           in terms of L⊙, mass of sun M⊙, ρ, particle radius r, and quality factor Q.

           iii. The quality factor is given by one of the following
           • If r ≪λ, Q ∼(r/λ)2
           • If r ∼λ, Q ∼1.
           • If r ≫λ, Q = 1

          Considering the three possible particle sizes, which is most likely to be blown away by
          the solar radiation pressure?

   b. The Poynting-Robertson eﬀect acts as another mechanism for cleaning out the solar sys-
     tem.

             i. Assume that a particle is in a circular orbit around the sun.  Find the speed of the
           particle v in terms of M⊙, distance from sun R, and any other fundamental constants.

            ii. Because the particle is moving, the radiation force is not directed directly away from the
           sun. Find the torque τ on the particle because of radiation pressure. You may assume
          that v ≪c.

           iii. Since τ = dL/dt, the angular momentum L of the particle changes with time. As such,
          develop a diﬀerential equation to ﬁnd dR/dt, the rate of change of the radial location of
          the particle. You may assume the orbit is always quasi circular.

         iv. Develop an expression for the time required to remove particles of size r ≈1 cm and
           density ρ ≈1000 kg/m3 originally in circular orbits at a distance R = Rearth, and use
          the numbers below to simplify your expression.

   Some useful constants include

                    M⊙   =  1.989 × 1030 kg
                        L⊙   =  3.828 × 1026 W
                                   Rearth =     1.5 × 1011 m
                              λmax  =         500 nm
```
