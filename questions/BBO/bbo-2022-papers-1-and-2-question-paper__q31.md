Part 3 of 10
In anaerobic glycolysis, 1 ATP is consumed to turn glucose into glucose-6-P. A second ATP is
then consumed in the next step. In the final steps, 4 ATP are produced. Thus anaerobic
glycolysis of glucose has a net yield of 2 (4 ATP produced - 2 ATP consumed).

Part 4 of 10
Addition of one glucose monomer to glycogen consumes 1 ATP molecule to phosphorylate
glucose and then 1 UTP molecule to power glycogen synthase.Phosphorylated glucose
(glucose-6-P) can be released from glycogen in an energetically favourable reaction which
does not produce or consume ATP. Glucose-6-P can be turned back into glucose in a second
energetically favourable reaction which does not produce or consume ATP.* Glucose-6-P can
be released into the cytoplasm of muscle cells and used directly in glycolysis.* Only glucose
can be released from liver cells into the blood, for use by muscle cells.
