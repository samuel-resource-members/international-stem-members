Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) is an enzyme that catalyses the conversion of pyruvate to lactate. Different organisms express LDH variants adapted to their thermal environment.

Scientists compared LDH from an Antarctic fish and a temperate fish. They measured relative enzyme activity across a range of temperatures.

LDH has a three-dimensional structure including:
- an active site, where substrate binds
- a hydrophobic core, stabilising the folded enzyme
- surface regions that interact with solvent and other molecules

Researchers modeled the structural effects of mutations in cold-adapted and warm-adapted LDH.

Examples include:
- replacing a buried hydrophobic residue with a charged one
- adding flexible residues (e.g., glycine)
- altering packing around the active site

Replacing a hydrophobic core residue with a charged one can destabilise protein folding.

Such a substitution could decrease catalytic efficiency.

A. TRUE
B. FALSE
