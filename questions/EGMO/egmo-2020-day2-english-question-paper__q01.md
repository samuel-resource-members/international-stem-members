```text
Problem 4. A permutation of the integers 1, 2,  . . . , m is called fresh  if there exists no positive
integer k < m such that the ﬁrst k numbers in the permutation are 1, 2, . . . , k in some order. Let fm
be the number of fresh permutations of the integers 1, 2, . . . , m.
Prove that fn             for all n        ≥n · fn−1       ≥3.
For example, if m = 4, then the permutation (3, 1, 4, 2) is fresh, whereas the permutation (2, 3, 1, 4) is
not.
```
