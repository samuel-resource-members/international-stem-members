Problem 3. We denote the number of positive divisors of a positive integer m by d(m) and the
number of distinct prime divisors of m by ω(m). Let k be a positive integer. Prove that there exist
inﬁnitely many positive integers n such that ω(n) = k and d(n) does not divide d(a2 + b2) for any
positive integers a, b satisfying a + b = n.
