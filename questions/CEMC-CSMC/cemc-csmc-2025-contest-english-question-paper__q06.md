3. Given a sequence S of real numbers b1, b2, b3, . . . , the complement of S is the sequence
   c1, c2, c3, . . . where c1 = 1 and for each integer n ≥2, cn satisfies

                          cn = b1cn−1 + b2cn−2 + · · · + bn−1c1

  That is, cn is the sum of all terms bkcn−k where k ranges from 1 to n −1.

    (a) The sequence S with bn = 2n−1 for each integer n ≥1 has terms 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, . . . .
       Determine the terms c2, c3, and c4 from the complement of S.
    (b) Prove that if S is a geometric sequence with complement c1, c2, c3, . . . , then
        there is a constant t such that cn = tcn−1 for all n ≥3.
    (c) Suppose that b1, b2, b3, . . .  is an arithmetic sequence and that its complement
         satisfies
                          c2025 = 3,   c2026 = 0,   c2027 = −3,   c2028 = 3

       Determine the value of b2025.

  A geometric sequence is a sequence in which each term after the first is obtained
   from the previous term by multiplying it by a non-zero constant, called the common
   ratio. For example, 3, 6, 12, 24 are the first four terms of a geometric sequence with
  common ratio 2.

  An arithmetic sequence is a sequence in which each term after the first is obtained
   from the previous term by adding a constant, called the common difference. For
   example, 2, 5, 8, 11 are the first four terms of an arithmetic sequence with common
   difference 3.
